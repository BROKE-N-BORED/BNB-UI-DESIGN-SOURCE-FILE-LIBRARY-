# 072 — Dark mode done right

> Entry 072 — added 2026-11-14 (Drop 6: Structure, comfort, and trust).

*A real dark theme is a second design, not the light theme with the colors flipped.*

**What it is.** A real dark theme is designed from the ground up against dark surfaces: a dark gray base instead of pure black, elevation shown by making higher surfaces lighter instead of casting shadows, accents desaturated so they stay legible, and text contrast checked on the lightest raised surface, not just on the background. Material Design's rule is the clearest statement of it: the recommended dark theme surface color is #121212, and higher surfaces get a semi-transparent white overlay, from 0 percent at the lowest level to 16 percent at the highest, so a card reads as raised because it is lighter than the page beneath it. Apple says the same thing from the other direction: dark colors are not necessarily inversions of their light counterparts, and apps should use semantic colors that adapt to the appearance rather than hard-coded values. GitHub's dark mode, shipped to more than 56 million developers in December 2020, was built the same way: not a color inversion but a token system, with the guiding principle that switching themes should feel like the same space in different lighting.

**Why it feels good.** A dark theme done right feels calm at night and easy to sit with for hours: the screen emits less light, the hierarchy still reads, and nothing glows or vibrates. A dark theme done wrong is worse than no dark theme at all. Pure black behind pure white text is the maximum possible contrast, 21:1, and it tires the eyes; saturated accents on a dark background shimmer and induce eye strain; and a theme made by inverting the light palette breaks every elevation cue, because shadows are invisible on black and the whole interface flattens into one dark slab. When the theme is designed as a second system, with its own surface ladder and its own desaturated accents, the app feels like it was always meant to be dark, and the user who lives in dark mode all day never has to think about it.

**The numbers.** Material Design 2's dark theme: base surface #121212; dark surfaces with 100 percent white body text reach a contrast level of at least 15.8:1, which is what guarantees body text still passes WCAG's 4.5:1 on the lightest raised surface. The elevation overlay table runs 0dp 0 percent, 1dp 5 percent, 2dp 7 percent, 3dp 8 percent, 4dp 9 percent, 6dp 11 percent, 8dp 12 percent, 12dp 14 percent, 16dp 15 percent, 24dp 16 percent. The baseline dark theme uses the 200 tone of the primary color, and Material recommends lighter tones (200 to 50) rather than the saturated tones (900 to 500) of the default theme. A branded dark surface can be made by overlaying the primary brand color at low opacity over #121212; Material's example, #121212 plus 8 percent primary, gives #1F1B24. True black is reserved for battery-critical OLED use, and Material warns that turning OLED pixels on and off can cause a delay when scrolling, making pixels blur. Apple asks for a contrast ratio no lower than 4.5:1 in all appearances and says to strive for 7:1 for custom foreground and background colors, especially in small text. On iOS and iPadOS, Apple uses two sets of background colors, base and elevated: base colors are dimmer so background interfaces recede, elevated colors are brighter so foreground interfaces advance, and the system switches between them automatically when an interface moves to the foreground. GitHub's dark mode shipped at GitHub Universe 2020, and the token architecture underneath it now powers more than 6 themes, including high-contrast and colorblind-friendly options.

**Where it fits his apps.** ACR: a dark theme suits long trace sessions, with the gold resonance accent desaturated to its 200 tone so it stays legible on #121212, and the chart background one step lighter than the page so the trace area reads as raised. MIMIK: a dark capture window keeps the preview the brightest thing on screen, with controls on a lighter raised surface. Qwkhnd and GITDATA: both are list-heavy, and a dark theme with a clear surface ladder (page, card, dialog) keeps rows, filters, and dialogs distinct without shadows. In Qt, a real dark theme is cheap to ship because theming is palette-based: one QPalette swap recolors every standard control, and the surface ladder is a handful of named colors in the token file. Two Qt facts still apply in the dark: the focus ring must be a border on :focus, since outline renders 0 pixels in this build, and a dialog that grows a section when the theme changes must scroll rather than clip.

**Watch out.** Do not invert the light theme: inversion keeps the light palette's saturated accents, which vibrate on dark surfaces, and it destroys the elevation model, since a shadow on a black background is invisible. Do not use pure black as the base: with #000000 there is no room to go darker, only lighter, and the surface ladder loses its bottom step. Do not check text contrast only against the background: the lightest raised surface is where body text is closest to failing, so check 4.5:1 there. Do not use light glows in place of dark shadows to show elevation: Material warns they do not represent a cast shadow and read wrong. Do not leave content images with white backgrounds glowing in a dark window: Apple advises softening them slightly. And do not offer an app-specific appearance toggle as the only control on a platform with a system setting: Apple warns users may think the app is broken when it ignores their systemwide choice, though on the Linux desktop, where no single system setting exists, an in-app toggle is the honest option.
