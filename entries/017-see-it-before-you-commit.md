# 017 — See it before you commit

> Entry 017 — added 2026-11-13 (Drop 2: Games, editors, and gadgets).

*hold to compare, hover to preview*

**What it is.** Two mechanisms, one principle: let the user see the result
before they commit to it. In Lightroom Classic, the backslash key shows
the before image; in Lightroom Mobile, press and hold the image to see
the original, and lifting your finger restores the edit. In Lightroom's
preset panel, hovering over a preset previews its effect on the photo,
and only a click applies it. The user never has to guess, apply, undo,
and try again.

**Why it feels good.** Editing is guessing until you can see it. A preview
turns a gamble into a look. Hold-to-compare answers "did I make it better
or worse" in one gesture. Hover-to-preview answers "what would this look
like" without touching anything. Both keep the user in flow: no dialogs,
no apply buttons, no undo stack to manage.

**The numbers.** No timing or size number in the sources. What they give
instead is the mechanism, and it is exact: Lightroom Classic, backslash
key for before; Lightroom Mobile, press and hold the image, lift to
restore the edit; presets preview on hover and apply on click, and are
shareable as .XMP files.

**Where it fits his apps.** Pixel Forge (hold to see the original image,
hover to preview a filter or preset), Reblend (hover a material preset
and see it on the object before applying), Data Pack Builder (preview a
pack's first rows before building the whole thing), Fyrn (hover a model
card to see its details without loading it).

**Watch out.** The preview must be instant or it reads as lag. On a
spinning drive, that means the preview image is cached, not rendered on
the fly. And hold-to-compare needs a visible hint, or nobody finds it:
a backslash key is invisible until someone teaches it. Pair it with
entry 014 (Teach by playing) and let the user discover it by doing.
